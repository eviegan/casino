What variance means in casino games

Variance is a statistical concept describing how widely individual outcomes can spread around an expected average.

In practical casino terms, variance helps explain why two games with similar long-run RTP can still feel completely different during ordinary play.

A simple low-vs-high variance example

Imagine two theoretical games with the same long-run RTP. Game A returns value more frequently through smaller payouts. Game B returns more of its value through rare but much larger payouts.

Game AGame B
Smaller swingsLarger swings
More regular small outcomesMore value concentrated in rarer outcomes
Lower variance profileHigher variance profile

Both could theoretically have the same RTP. The difference is how widely results are distributed.

How variance appears in slot design

Variance can be influenced by many elements of a slot's mathematics:

  • base-game symbol values;
  • frequency of free spins;
  • large multipliers;
  • jackpot probability;
  • maximum-win combinations;
  • bonus-feature frequency;
  • how much RTP is concentrated inside special features.

A slot that places a large portion of theoretical return inside a rare bonus feature can create much bigger short-term swings than a game where more value is distributed through ordinary spins.

Why short sessions can be misleading

Variance is one reason a short session should never be treated as proof that a game is “paying well” or “not paying.” Random sequences can temporarily move far away from the mathematical average.

The smaller the sample, the more noise there is. Long losing streaks and strong winning streaks are both possible even when the game is operating exactly as designed.

Frequently asked questions

Is variance the same as volatility?

They are closely related, but variance is the formal statistical concept while volatility is the player-facing description commonly used by slot providers.

Does high variance mean a game pays more?

No. Variance describes the spread of outcomes, not the game's total theoretical return.

Can two slots have the same RTP and different variance?

Yes. Similar RTP figures can be produced by very different payout structures.

Why does variance matter?

It helps explain why short-term results can differ sharply from long-term theoretical expectations.

Does higher variance mean bigger maximum wins?

Not necessarily. Maximum win and variance are separate game characteristics.
