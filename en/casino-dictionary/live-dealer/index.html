What is a live dealer casino?

A live dealer game is played at a real or studio-based gaming table with a human dealer, while players participate remotely through a live video stream and digital betting interface.

Common live games include blackjack, roulette, baccarat, poker variants and game-show-style titles.

How a live dealer round works

1

Betting Opens

Players place digital bets during a timed betting window.

2

Dealer Acts

The dealer spins the wheel, deals cards or runs the physical game.

3

Result Captured

Optical recognition or table technology records the physical result.

4

Bets Settle

The platform settles eligible player bets according to the rules.

Technology behind the table

Live casino platforms use several technologies together:

  • multi-camera video streaming;
  • low-latency delivery;
  • game control units;
  • optical character recognition or sensor systems;
  • digital betting interfaces;
  • real-time settlement systems.

The goal is to synchronize the physical table result with every remote player's digital bet.

Standard tables vs VIP tables

Providers often offer multiple tables for the same game with different betting limits, languages, studio themes or dealer schedules.

VIP tables generally use higher minimum and maximum stakes and may be targeted at high-value players.

Behind the camera: a live casino is closer to a TV studio than a website

Large live-casino studios combine gaming tables with professional lighting, multiple cameras, microphones, control systems and real-time streaming infrastructure. The dealer runs the physical game while software overlays betting controls, statistics, timers and results for remote players.

Major provider Evolution describes its live studios as advanced TV/film-style environments broadcasting casino games in real time. That is a useful mental model: the table is physical, but the distribution and player interaction layer is digital.

Why latency matters

A live game has to keep the video, betting timer and recorded result synchronized. If a player's connection is delayed, the betting interface must still close before the physical result is known. This is why live games use clearly defined “bets closed” moments.

Some providers also offer multiple camera angles, game histories, roadmaps in baccarat, roulette statistics and chat—all layered around the live video feed.

Frequently asked questions

Are live dealer games played by real people?

Yes. A human dealer operates the physical table while players participate online.

Do live dealer games use RNG?

Traditional live tables usually derive the result from physical cards, wheels or equipment, although digital systems support the broadcast and settlement process.

Why is there a betting timer?

The timer ensures all remote bets are locked before the dealer completes the next physical action.

Are live dealer games slower than digital games?

Usually yes because they follow a real-time dealer pace.

Can live tables have different limits?

Yes. Minimum and maximum stakes vary by table.
