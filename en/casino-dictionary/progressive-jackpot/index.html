Progressive jackpots can grow over time instead of remaining at one fixed value, sometimes linking play across multiple games or operators.

What makes a jackpot progressive?

A progressive jackpot is a prize whose displayed value can increase according to the rules of the jackpot system until it is won or reset.

Unlike a fixed jackpot, the visible meter changes over time. The exact growth mechanism depends on the provider and the jackpot network.

How a progressive jackpot grows

1

Seed Value

The jackpot begins from a predefined starting amount.

2

Contribution

A defined contribution associated with qualifying activity feeds the jackpot meter.

3

Trigger

A qualifying event awards the jackpot.

4

Reset

The jackpot normally returns to its seed value and starts growing again.

A common misconception is that the player's full stake goes into the jackpot. Usually only a defined portion associated with qualifying play contributes to the progressive pool.

What is a jackpot seed value?

The seed is the predefined amount to which the jackpot normally resets after it is won.

For example, a progressive jackpot might grow from a $100,000 seed to $1,250,000. After the qualifying jackpot win, the meter may reset to $100,000 and begin increasing again.

How progressive jackpots can be won

There is no universal trigger. A progressive jackpot may be awarded through a specific symbol combination, a random feature, a dedicated jackpot bonus, a side bet, a pick-and-win feature or a mystery event.

Some jackpots require a minimum stake, while others allow all eligible stakes to participate.

Common progressive jackpot misconceptions

“A huge meter means it is due.” Not necessarily. A growing meter alone does not imply that the next trigger is more likely.

“Higher stake always gives better odds.” Only if the rules explicitly define the probability that way.

“Every jackpot keeps increasing forever.” Some systems use must-win thresholds, fixed upper ranges or other special mechanics.

Progressive jackpots and RTP

Part of a progressive game's theoretical return can be allocated to the jackpot component. This means the base-game payout pattern may not tell the full story about the game's total theoretical return.

When comparing progressive and non-progressive slots, check both the displayed RTP and the jackpot rules.

Frequently asked questions

What is a seed value?

The seed value is the predefined amount to which a progressive jackpot normally resets after being won.

Can one progressive jackpot link multiple casinos?

Yes. Some network progressive jackpots connect qualifying play across participating operators.

Does a high jackpot meter mean the jackpot is due?

Not automatically. The meter size alone does not necessarily change the trigger probability.

Is every jackpot progressive?

No. Fixed jackpots have a predefined prize value rather than a continuously growing meter.

Does the full bet amount go into the jackpot?

Usually not. Only a defined contribution associated with qualifying play typically feeds the progressive pool.
