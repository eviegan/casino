Wagering sounds simple—place a bet and that amount counts as wagering—but the word is used differently when casinos discuss turnover, bonuses, missions and player activity.

What wagering means

Wagering generally refers to the value of bets placed on casino games or other eligible betting products. It is not the same as depositing money, withdrawing money or losing money.

If you deposit $100 and place ten $10 bets, your deposit is $100 but your total wagering is $100. If you keep recycling winnings into new bets, your wagering can become much larger than your original deposit.

Deposit vs wagering vs turnover

TermMeaning
DepositMoney added to the casino account
WageringTotal eligible amount staked on bets
TurnoverOften used as another term for total amount wagered
LossThe net amount lost after wins and losses are combined

A player can wager $5,000 while only depositing $200 if funds are repeatedly won, re-bet and recycled through many rounds.

What is a wagering requirement?

A wagering requirement is a promotional condition requiring a specified amount of eligible betting before certain bonus funds or related winnings become withdrawable.

Example: A $50 bonus with a 20× bonus wagering requirement could require $1,000 of eligible wagering before the promotional requirement is completed. Exact formulas vary, so always check whether the multiplier applies to the bonus only, deposit + bonus, or another base amount.

Why not every game contributes equally

Promotions can apply different contribution rates to different games. Slots may contribute 100%, while some table games may contribute less or nothing toward the requirement.

This prevents players from assuming that every $10 bet reduces the remaining wagering balance by the same $10.

Follow the money: one deposit, many wagers

Here is where wagering becomes more interesting. Imagine a player deposits $100 and makes a $10 slot bet. The bet wins $18, so the balance becomes $108. The player then wagers another $10, wins $5, and continues playing. Every new $10 stake adds another $10 to turnover—even when the money being wagered originally came from an earlier win.

After 50 rounds at $10 each, total wagering is $500, even though the original deposit was only $100. This is why deposit volume, wagering volume and player profit/loss should never be treated as the same metric.

Interesting use case: Casino missions often say “Wager $500” rather than “Deposit $500” because wagering measures actual game activity, while a deposit only measures money entering the account.

How to decode a wagering promotion in 30 seconds

When you see a wagering-based offer, identify five numbers: the qualifying amount, multiplier, eligible games, contribution percentage and deadline. Then check whether there is a maximum bet while the promotion is active.

How to read wagering professionally

The word can describe ordinary betting activity or a promotional play-through condition, so context matters.

1

Identify the metric: total stakes, eligible turnover, bonus wagering progress and deposits are different numbers.

2

For promotions, identify the base: a multiplier can apply to bonus funds, deposit + bonus, winnings or another stated amount.

3

Check contribution: not every game or bet may reduce a promotional requirement at the same rate.

4

Check restrictions: expiry, maximum bet and withdrawal conditions can materially change a wagering offer.

Frequently asked questions

Does wagering mean losing?

No. Wagering is the amount staked, regardless of whether those bets win or lose.

Can wagering be higher than deposits?

Yes. Re-betting winnings can make total wagering much larger than deposited funds.

What does 20× wagering mean?

It means a specified base amount must generally be wagered twenty times, but the exact base amount depends on the promotion.

Do all games count toward wagering requirements?

Not always. Contribution rates vary by promotion and game category.

Is turnover the same as wagering?

Often yes in casino reporting, although terminology can vary by operator.
