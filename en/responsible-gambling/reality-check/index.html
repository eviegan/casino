A reality check is a session reminder designed to interrupt continuous gambling and bring attention back to elapsed time or other session information.

1. What a reality check is

A reality check is an on-screen reminder shown during gambling. Its purpose is to interrupt continuous play and make session information more visible instead of allowing time to pass unnoticed.

2. What the reminder may show

Depending on the operator and rules, a reminder may display elapsed time and provide options such as continuing, reviewing account information or leaving the game. Exact implementations vary.

3. Why interruptions can be useful

Continuous interfaces reduce natural stopping points. A deliberate interruption creates a moment to reassess whether the session still fits the plan you made before starting.

4. Decide your response before it appears

Do not wait for the reminder to decide what it means. For example, you might decide that the first reminder means a five-minute break and the second means the session ends.

5. Notice if you dismiss every reminder instantly

A tool has little value if it becomes a button you automatically close. Repeatedly dismissing reminders without checking your behaviour may mean you need a firmer time boundary.

6. Combine reminders with money and time limits

Reality checks are prompts, not hard spending controls. They are stronger when combined with deposit limits, planned stop times and regular account-history reviews.

7. When reminders are no longer enough

If reminders do not interrupt harmful patterns, consider a timeout, self-exclusion or support rather than relying on increasingly frequent pop-ups.

Using a reality check more effectively

A reminder is useful only if it creates a genuine decision point. Decide in advance what information you will review and what action will follow.

1

Set a meaningful interval. Use a frequency that interrupts continuous play before time becomes easy to lose track of.

2

Read before dismissing. Check elapsed time and account information instead of automatically closing the message.

3

Pre-plan the response. For example, one reminder may trigger a break and a later reminder may end the session.

4

Move to stronger controls if reminders stop working. Timeouts, financial limits or exclusion may be more suitable when reminders are routinely ignored.

Frequently asked questions

What is a gambling reality check?

It is an on-screen session reminder intended to interrupt continuous play and make elapsed time or other session information more noticeable.

Does a reality check force me to stop?

Not necessarily. Implementations vary, and many reality checks are reminders rather than hard session stops.

How often should reality checks appear?

There is no universal interval suitable for everyone. Choose or use intervals that create meaningful stopping points and follow applicable platform rules.

What if I always dismiss reality checks?

If reminders no longer affect your decisions, consider stronger boundaries such as a fixed stop time, timeout or self-exclusion.

Important: Responsible-gambling tools and self-exclusion rules vary by operator and jurisdiction. Use the current rules shown by the licensed operator or relevant regulator.
