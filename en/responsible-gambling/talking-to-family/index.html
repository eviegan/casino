Talking to Family

Understand the tool, recognise the warning signs and know what practical action can come next.

PLANCHECKACT

Talking to family about gambling can feel difficult, but a clear and honest conversation can reduce secrecy and help turn concern into practical action.

1. Prepare before the conversation

Choose one or two people you trust and decide what you want them to understand. Gather basic facts about money, debts, accounts or gambling patterns if those details are relevant.

2. Choose a calm, private moment

Avoid starting the conversation during an argument, immediately after a gambling loss or when either person is rushed. A calmer setting gives everyone more room to listen and respond.

3. Start with the truth, not a perfect explanation

You do not need a polished speech. Explain what has been happening, what worries you and what you want to change. Avoid minimising debts or promising outcomes you cannot guarantee.

4. Be clear about financial impact

If household money has been affected, honesty matters. Explain known debts, missed bills or borrowing and identify urgent essentials that need protection. Financial surprises discovered later can make trust harder to rebuild.

5. Ask for specific support

Useful requests might include sitting with you while you self-exclude, helping review a budget, holding you accountable to a plan, or simply checking in at agreed times. Family members should not have to become permanent financial police.

6. Respect the family member’s boundaries too

Gambling harm can affect partners, parents, children and friends. They may need their own financial boundaries and support. Listening to their concerns is part of rebuilding trust.

7. Treat the first conversation as a beginning

One conversation rarely fixes everything. Agree on the next practical action, such as blocking gambling access, contacting support, protecting bills or arranging another conversation after the immediate situation is clearer.

Frequently asked questions

How do I tell my family I have a gambling problem?

Choose a calm private moment, explain what has been happening honestly, describe the impact and ask for specific practical support.

Should I tell them the full amount of gambling debt?

If finances affect them or the household, accurate information is important. If you are unsure of the total, say that clearly and work on establishing the facts.

What if my family becomes angry?

Anger, fear or disappointment can be understandable reactions. Focus on safety, honesty and the next practical steps rather than expecting the issue to be resolved in one conversation.

Can family members get support too?

Yes. Gambling harm can affect relatives and partners as well as the person gambling, and specialist services may offer support for affected family and friends.
