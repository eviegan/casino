Self Exclusion

Understand the tool, recognise the warning signs and know what practical action can come next.

PLANCHECKACT

Self-exclusion is a formal step for someone who wants support to stop gambling by asking gambling businesses or schemes to block access for a defined period.

1. What self-exclusion means

Self-exclusion is a formal arrangement intended to support a period without gambling. Depending on the jurisdiction and scheme, it may apply to one operator, multiple operators, online gambling, physical venues or a combination.

2. When to consider self-exclusion

It may be appropriate when gambling is causing harm, when ordinary limits are repeatedly bypassed, when attempts to stop have not lasted, or when easy account access makes urges harder to manage.

3. What usually happens after a request

The operator or scheme records the exclusion, restricts gambling access covered by the arrangement and applies its procedures for preventing further play. Exact periods and rules vary by jurisdiction.

4. Operator vs multi-operator exclusion

An operator exclusion covers that business or group according to its rules. Multi-operator schemes can extend protection across participating gambling businesses, reducing the need to contact each one separately.

5. Add more than one barrier

Self-exclusion can be reinforced with gambling-blocking software, bank gambling blocks, removal of gambling apps and marketing, and support from someone you trust.

6. Deal with practical finances too

Stopping access does not automatically resolve debts or household financial pressure. Protect essential bills, review accounts and seek appropriate debt or financial advice if needed.

7. Do not treat the end date as a target

The expiry of an exclusion does not mean someone should automatically return to gambling. Review what has changed, how you are feeling and whether continued exclusion is the safer choice.

Frequently asked questions

What is gambling self-exclusion?

It is a formal arrangement that supports a person who wants to stop gambling by restricting their access to covered gambling services for a defined period.

Does one self-exclusion cover every gambling company?

Not always. Coverage depends on the operator, jurisdiction and whether a multi-operator scheme is used.

Can I use other blocking tools at the same time?

Yes. Device blocking tools, bank gambling blocks and support from trusted people can create additional barriers.

Should I automatically start gambling when an exclusion ends?

No. An end date is not a recommendation to resume. Consider whether continued exclusion or further support is more appropriate.

Important: Responsible-gambling tools and self-exclusion rules vary by operator and jurisdiction. Use the current rules shown by the licensed operator or relevant regulator.
