Support organizations can provide information, confidential conversations, referrals, counselling, peer support and practical tools—but services and eligibility differ by country.

1. Different organizations provide different kinds of help

Some services focus on confidential helplines, others on counselling or treatment, peer support, family support, financial guidance, self-exclusion or referral to local services. One organization does not need to provide every type of support.

2. Check that the service is legitimate

Use official organization websites, recognised health services, regulators or established charities to verify contact details. Be cautious with websites that imitate support services while primarily promoting gambling products.

3. Support is usually country-specific

Legal systems, healthcare access, self-exclusion schemes and helpline networks differ between countries. A strong international article should therefore help readers locate the correct local service rather than assuming one national number applies everywhere.

4. Examples of established support organizations

In the United States, the National Council on Problem Gambling provides information and connections to local resources. In Great Britain, GamCare provides gambling-harm support services including the National Gambling Helpline. Readers elsewhere should use recognised local health, regulatory or gambling-support services for their country.

5. Many services support affected family members too

Partners, parents, children and friends may need help with boundaries, communication, finances and emotional impact. Many gambling-support organizations recognise that gambling harm can affect more than the person who gambles.

6. Gambling support may need to connect with financial support

When debt, missed bills or borrowing are involved, gambling-specific help may need to be combined with independent debt or financial advice. Addressing the gambling behaviour without stabilising essential finances can leave an important part of the problem unresolved.

7. What to say when you first contact a service

You can keep the first message simple: explain that gambling is causing concern, describe the main impact and ask what support options are available. You do not need to prepare a complete history before making first contact.

How to assess a support organization

A useful support page should help readers distinguish genuine help services from websites that primarily market gambling products.

1

Check who runs the service. Prefer established charities, healthcare providers, regulators or recognized specialist organizations.

2

Confirm the location served. Helplines, eligibility and self-exclusion systems are often country-specific.

3

Review the type of support. A service may offer counselling, peer support, family support, financial guidance or referrals rather than every service in one place.

4

Verify contact information on the official site. Do not rely on copied phone numbers or unofficial directories.

Frequently asked questions

Are gambling support organizations only for people with a diagnosis?

No. Many services support people who are worried about their gambling or someone else’s gambling before any formal diagnosis exists.

Can family members contact support organizations?

Yes. Many services provide information or direct support for people affected by another person’s gambling.

Are support services the same in every country?

No. Availability, eligibility, funding and self-exclusion systems vary by location, so readers should verify the official service for their jurisdiction.

How can I check whether a support organization is genuine?

Use official government, health-service, regulator or established charity sources and verify contact details on the organization’s own website.
