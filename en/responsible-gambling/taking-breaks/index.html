Taking Breaks From Gambling

Understand the issue, review practical options and know what action can come next.

PAUSERESETREVIEW

A break is most useful when it creates real distance from gambling rather than simply interrupting one session for a few minutes.

1. Why breaks matter

Continuous play can make it harder to notice time, spending, emotions and changing decisions. Stepping away creates space to review what has happened without the pressure of the next bet or game round.

2. Short breaks during a session

A short break can mean leaving the screen, standing up, drinking water, checking the time and reviewing current spending. The goal is to interrupt automatic play and make the decision to continue a conscious one.

3. Longer time-outs

A longer break may last a day, several days or longer depending on the tools available. This is different from simply closing a browser tab because it creates a planned period in which gambling access is intentionally reduced.

4. Make the break harder to undo

Remove saved payment methods, uninstall apps, mute gambling notifications and avoid promotional messages where possible. If the break can be cancelled instantly, it may not create enough distance during an impulsive moment.

5. Use the break to review patterns

Look at account history, money spent, time spent and the emotions that tend to trigger gambling. A break is useful not only because play stops, but because it creates an opportunity to understand what has been happening.

6. Decide in advance what happens after the break

Before a break ends, decide whether you intend to return, extend the break or use stronger safeguards. If the same harmful pattern resumes immediately, that is important information rather than a reason to keep repeating the same approach.

7. When a break may not be enough

If you repeatedly override time-outs, gamble on other sites during breaks or return to the same harmful behaviour, self-exclusion and specialist support may be more appropriate than relying on short breaks alone.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a gambling break be?

There is no single correct length. The useful length depends on the pattern you are trying to interrupt and the tools available to you.

Is closing the app the same as taking a break?

Not necessarily. A meaningful break is usually planned and creates enough distance that returning requires a deliberate decision.

What should I do during a gambling break?

Use the time for normal routines, sleep, exercise, social contact, financial review or support conversations rather than replacing gambling with another high-intensity activity.

What if I keep cancelling my breaks?

Repeatedly undoing breaks suggests that stronger barriers or external support may be more useful than relying on self-control alone.
