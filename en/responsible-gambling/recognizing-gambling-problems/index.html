Recognizing Gambling Problems

Understand the tool, recognise the warning signs and know what practical action can come next.

Gambling problems are often recognised through patterns: loss of control, increasing preoccupation, financial pressure, secrecy, chasing losses and harm to everyday life.

1. Problems are usually patterns, not one bad day

A single loss does not by itself define a gambling problem. More useful warning signs involve repeated changes in control, priorities, finances, relationships and emotional wellbeing.

2. Gambling takes up more mental space

Thinking constantly about bets, planning the next session or checking results during work and family time can indicate that gambling is becoming increasingly central.

3. Chasing losses changes the purpose of play

Chasing happens when the focus shifts toward winning back money already lost. This can lead to longer sessions, larger stakes and decisions driven by urgency rather than a pre-set budget.

4. Financial warning signs

Borrowing to gamble, missing bills, selling possessions, hiding transactions or repeatedly moving money intended for essentials are serious signals that gambling is affecting financial stability.

5. Secrecy and relationship changes

Lying about time or money spent, withdrawing from family, breaking promises or becoming defensive when gambling is discussed can damage trust and increase isolation.

6. Mood, sleep and daily functioning matter

Stress, anxiety, irritability, sleep disruption, difficulty concentrating or neglecting normal activities can accompany harmful gambling patterns.

7. What to do when you recognise several signs

Stop and create distance from gambling, protect access to money, use available blocking or exclusion tools, tell someone you trust and consider specialist support. You do not need to wait for a crisis before acting.

A practical warning-sign review

No single sign proves the whole picture. What matters is whether several changes are appearing together and whether gambling continues despite harm.

1

Control: Are sessions or stakes repeatedly going beyond what was planned?

2

Consequences: Are money, work, sleep, relationships or normal responsibilities being affected?

3

Secrecy: Is gambling being hidden, minimized or explained differently to different people?

4

Persistence: Does gambling continue even after repeated attempts to stop, limit or repair the consequences?

Frequently asked questions

What are common signs of gambling problems?

Common signs include preoccupation, chasing losses, secrecy, borrowing money, difficulty stopping, withdrawal from normal activities and gambling despite financial or relationship harm.

Does losing a lot of money automatically mean someone has a gambling problem?

Not by itself. The broader pattern of control, behaviour, consequences and continued gambling is important.

Is chasing losses a serious warning sign?

Yes. Chasing losses can drive longer sessions and greater financial risk.

Should someone wait until gambling becomes a crisis before getting help?

No. Support can be useful as soon as gambling begins affecting money, relationships, wellbeing or the ability to stop as planned.
