A fixed jackpot stays at a defined value; a progressive jackpot can grow. That simple difference changes how the prize is funded, displayed, reset and sometimes triggered.

Where does progressive growth come from?

A progressive jackpot usually receives a defined contribution associated with qualifying activity. The meter grows until the jackpot is won or another rule causes a reset.

A fixed jackpot does not need a growing contribution pool because the prize amount is defined by the game rules.

Why the seed value matters

A progressive jackpot rarely resets to zero. Instead, it returns to a seed value—the starting amount funded by the system.

If a jackpot grows from $100,000 to $1.5 million, a win may reset it to $100,000, after which contributions begin building the meter again.

Is a huge progressive jackpot more likely to pay?

Not automatically. A high meter means the prize is larger, not necessarily that the trigger probability has increased.

Some special “must-win-by” jackpots use upper thresholds, but that is a specific mechanic and should not be assumed for all progressive systems.

Which jackpot is easier to understand?

Fixed jackpots are simpler because the prize value is known. Progressive jackpots are more dynamic and can become much larger, but players need to understand contribution, eligibility, trigger and reset rules.

Frequently asked questions

What is a fixed jackpot?

A jackpot with a predetermined prize value rather than a growing meter.

What is a progressive jackpot?

A jackpot whose value can increase according to the system rules.

What is a seed value?

The amount a progressive jackpot normally resets to after being won.

Is a large progressive jackpot due to pay?

Not automatically. Prize size and trigger probability are separate unless the rules say otherwise.

Can fixed jackpots be very large?

Yes. Fixed describes how the value behaves, not necessarily whether the prize is small.
