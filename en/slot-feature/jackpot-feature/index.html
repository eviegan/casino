A jackpot feature awards a defined top-tier prize under specific game rules. Jackpots can be fixed, progressive, local or networked, and the trigger can be random or tied to symbols, picks, wheels or other feature mechanics.

What is a jackpot feature?

A slot jackpot is a top-tier prize governed by the game's jackpot rules. Its value may be fixed or progressive, and its trigger can be random or feature-based.

Main jackpot types

FIX

Fixed jackpot

Pays a predefined amount or multiplier under the game rules.

↑

Progressive jackpot

The displayed prize can grow until it is won and then resets.

L

Local progressive

The pool is limited to a particular operator, group of games or environment.

N

Network progressive

A wider pool can connect qualifying activity across multiple games or operators.

How jackpots can be triggered

RNG

Random trigger

Some jackpots can drop independently of a visible symbol pattern.

PICK

Pick feature

A player can reveal items to determine a jackpot tier.

SYM

Symbol trigger

Specific symbols or combinations can open a jackpot round.

WHL

Wheel feature

A wheel or selector can determine the awarded jackpot level.

What the jackpot meter actually means

A displayed jackpot value shows the current prize amount. It does not mean that the jackpot is “due,” easier to hit or predictable from how long it has been growing.

Useful distinction: Prize size and trigger probability are separate concepts. A larger displayed meter does not automatically mean a higher chance of winning it on the next spin.

Frequently asked questions

What is a jackpot feature in a slot?

It is a mechanic connected to a top-tier prize that is awarded when the game's jackpot conditions are met.

What is the difference between a fixed and progressive jackpot?

A fixed jackpot has a defined value, while a progressive jackpot can grow until it is won and reset.

Does a jackpot meter mean the jackpot is due?

No. The meter shows prize value, not that the jackpot is predictable or due to hit.

Are jackpot triggers the same in every slot?

No. They can be random or tied to symbols, picks, wheels or other game-specific mechanics.
